## Página 2 --- Clinical Measurements

**Clinical Measurements:** Uses sensors, transducers, and chemical
measurement principles to obtain information from a sample.

**Instrument:** Equipment or device that transforms a physical variable
of interest (measuring) into a form that can be recorded (quantity).

### Diagrama de la muestra

A partir de una **Sample** se plantean dos tipos de preguntas:

**Identification** - What is it? - What are its components? -
Recognition of the substances present in the sample. - **Qualitative
analysis**

**Quantification** - How much of each component is there? - Quantitative
measurement of a component in the sample. - **Quantitative analysis**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Página 3 --- Analytical Measurement Process

### Flujo del proceso

**Analytical problem → Preliminary operations → Analytical measurement →
Data processing → Results**

El bloque **Sampling** está asociado al planteamiento del problema
analítico.

A set of operations that begin with the statement of the problem:

-   What components are present in a sample?
-   What is the structure of a given component?
-   In what concentration is it present?

and end with the obtaining of results that satisfy the conditions of the
problem stated.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Página 4 --- Definitions

El diagrama superior conserva el flujo:

**Analytical problem → Preliminary operations → Analytical measurement →
Data processing → Results**, con **Sampling** asociado al problema
analítico.

**Sample:** Representative portion of the material or object under
study.

**Analyte:** Component of interest present in the sample from which some
type of information is desired: presence, concentration, state, etc. The
remaining components are referred to as the matrix. Substances that may
introduce a systematic error in the analysis result are referred to as
interferents or interferences.

**Analysis:** Physical or chemical process by which information is
obtained about the constituents of a sample. E.g. Blood analysis, water
analysis, food analysis, etc.

**Determination:** Performing the necessary operations during the
analysis process to obtain information about the identity, quantity, or
properties of the analyte of interest. E.g. Determination of phosphates,
determination of acidity index, etc.

**Performing the determination:** requires measuring one or more
physical or chemical properties of the analytes. E.g., measuring
electrical potential, absorbance, emission intensity, etc.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Página 5 --- Other terms related to analysis

Se conserva el esquema del proceso analítico:

**Analytical problem → Preliminary operations → Analytical measurement →
Data processing → Results**, con **Sampling**.

**Analytical technique:** A physical or chemical principle that can be
used to analyze a sample or obtain information about an analyte.
Analytical techniques include, for example, gravimetry or
ultraviolet-visible spectroscopy.

**Method:** The concrete application of an analytical technique for the
analysis of a defined sample and the acquisition of specific
information. It includes all the operations involved until the final
result is obtained.

Examples: gravimetric determination of sulfates in water;
spectrophotometric determination of phenol index in water; Mohr method
for the determination of chlorides in water, etc.

**Procedure:** A set of written guidelines that explain how to apply a
method to a particular sample.

## Página 7 --- Properties of Analytical Methods: Precision

**Precision:** is the degree of agreement between the results of a
series of repeated measurements. It depends on the magnitude of random
errors.

Precision can be measured from results obtained by repeating the
measurement under conditions of minimal variability (applying the same
method to the same material, under the same conditions, in the same work
session), which is referred to as **repeatability**.

Alternatively, when the method is applied under conditions of greater
variability (different equipment, reagents, operators, work shifts,
etc.), it is referred to as **reproducibility**.

## Página 9 --- Properties of Analytical Methods: Detection and Quantification

La figura divide la concentración del analito en:

**Non detection → LD → Region of detection → LQ → Region of
quantification**

Eje: **Analyte Concentration**

**Limit of Detection (LoD):** The limit of detection is the lowest
concentration of the analyte in a sample that an analytical method can
distinguish from a zero-concentration sample (blank).

**Limit of Quantitation (LoQ):** The limit of quantification is the
lowest concentration of an analyte that an analytical method can
quantify with an acceptable degree of accuracy and precision.

Below this value, it is not possible to provide a reliable quantitative
result; only the presence of the analyte can be detected.

## Página 10 --- Properties of Analytical Methods: Nonlinear nature and limits

### Nonlinear nature of analytical methods

Analytical methods are generally considered to be cubic or sigmoidal in
nature. At the low end of quantitation no signal can be detected, then
as the signal increases, the detection and quantitation can be reliably
determined, a linear transition follows and finally saturation of the
method.

From these curves, **LOB, LOD, and LOQ** of the method can be
determined.

La figura muestra una curva sigmoidal: zona baja prácticamente plana,
transición ascendente y saturación.

### Multiple approaches for limit determination

**1. Based on the standard deviation of the blank**

This method of limit determination uses the mean and standard deviation
of blank samples to set the limits. If normal distributions are not
likely, other distributions may be used using the same K sigma
multiplier. Sample sizes should be selected based on delta, power, and
alpha. Normally 10 or more determinations are made at no concentration
in the appropriate matrix.

$$LOB = Mean\ blank + 1.645 \times Stdev\ blank\ (one\ sided\ 95\%)$$

$$LOD = Mean\ blank + 3.3 \times Stdev\ blank\ (one\ sided\ 95\% \times 2)$$

$$LOQ = Mean\ blank + 10 \times Stdev\ blank\ (one\ sided\ 95\% \times 6)$$

La figura muestra distribuciones gaussianas para blank samples y las
posiciones de **LOB**, **LOD** y **LOQ**.

## Página 11 --- Properties of Analytical Methods: Standard deviation of response and slope

**1. Based on standard deviation of the response and the slope**

This approach to limit determination is good when the method does not
exhibit background noise of any magnitude. A standard curve may be used
to determine LOD and LOQ. Samples need to be taken in the range of the
LOD and LOQ and not extrapolated into the range. Normally six or more
determinations are made at five concentrations.

$$LOD = \frac{3.3\sigma}{Slope}$$

$$LOQ = \frac{10\sigma}{Slope}$$

Where:

-   $\sigma$ = the standard deviation of the response at low
    concentrations
-   Slope = the slope of the calibration curve.

### Términos y definiciones mostrados

**Desviación estándar absoluta, $s$:**

$$s = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_i-\bar{x})^2}{N-1}}$$

**Desviación estándar relativa (RSD):**

$$RSD = \frac{s}{\bar{x}}$$

**Desviación estándar de la media, $s_m$:**

$$s_m = \frac{s}{\sqrt{N}}$$

**Coeficiente de variación, CV:**

$$CV = \frac{s}{\bar{x}}\times100\%$$

**Varianza:**

$$s^2$$

-   $x_i$ = valor numérico de la iésima medida.
-   $\bar{x}$ = media de $N$ medidas.

$$\bar{x}=\frac{\sum x_i}{N}$$

## Página 17 --- Calibration of Measuring Instruments: Step 2

### Step 2

**Establish a mathematical model that best describes the output-input
relationship of the instrument.**

**Plot the experimental points obtained on a pattern vs. results diagram
and calculate their general trend using the least squares method.**

La gráfica se titula:

**CÁLCULO DE LA LÍNEA RECTA DE MEJOR AJUSTE POR EL MÉTODO DE MÍNIMOS
CUADRADOS**

Modelo:

$$y=mx+b$$

Pendiente:

$$m=\frac{n\sum xy-(\sum x)(\sum y)}{n\sum x^2-(\sum x)^2}$$

La diapositiva define $m$ como la **pendiente**, es decir, el número de
unidades que aumenta $y$ por cada unidad de $x$.

Ordenada al origen:

$$b=\frac{(\sum y)(\sum x^2)-(\sum x)(\sum xy)}{n\sum x^2-(\sum x)^2}$$

Se define $b$ como la **ordenada al origen; intersección de y; punto
donde la recta corta el eje y; valor de y cuando x = 0**.

Coeficiente de correlación:

$$r=\frac{n\sum xy-(\sum x)(\sum y)}{\sqrt{[n\sum x^2-(\sum x)^2][n\sum y^2-(\sum y)^2]}}$$

Se indica que $r$ es el **coeficiente de correlación**, varía de 0 a 1;
cuanto más cerca de 1, más se ajusta la recta a los puntos
experimentales; entre más cerca de 1, mayor es la bondad de ajuste.

## Página 28 --- Dynamic Characteristics

The dynamic characteristics of a system are the characteristic responses
to varying inputs over time.

The relationship between the input and output of any linear and
time-invariant measurement system can be written as:

$$a_n\frac{d^ny(t)}{dt^n}+a_{n-1}\frac{d^{n-1}y(t)}{dt^{n-1}}+\cdots+a_1\frac{dy(t)}{dt}+a_0y(t)$$

$$=b_m\frac{d^mx(t)}{dt^m}+b_{m-1}\frac{d^{m-1}x(t)}{dt^{m-1}}+\cdots+b_1\frac{dx(t)}{dt}+b_0x(t)$$

## Página 34 --- Dynamic Characteristics: Higher-order sensors

**Higher-order sensors**

La figura presenta una respuesta temporal oscilatoria alrededor del
valor de estado estacionario, con referencias en aproximadamente 0.1,
0.9 y 1, además de una banda de tolerancia **±Δ% (2--5%)**.

Se identifican gráficamente:

-   $t_d$: delay time
-   $t_r$: rise time
-   $t_s$: response/settling time
-   Overshoot $\alpha$
-   Atenuación entre picos consecutivos

### Definiciones

**Rise time $t_r$:** the required time that the steady-state value rises
from 10% to 90%.

**Response time $t_s$:** the required time that the output response
maintains stability in the ±Δ% error tolerances.

**Overshoot $\alpha$:** the value that the maximum output exceeds the
steady-state value.

**Delay time $t_d$:** the required time that the output first reaches
50% of steady-state value.

**Attenuation $\psi$:** the percentage of height decline between two
adjacent peaks (or troughs),

$$\psi=\frac{a-a_1}{a}\times100\%$$
